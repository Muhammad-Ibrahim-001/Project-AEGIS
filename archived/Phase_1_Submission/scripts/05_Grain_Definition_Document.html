1. Kimball Grain Principles & Granularity Foundations

In the Kimball dimensional modeling methodology, declaring the grain is the single most critical step in designing a dimensional data warehouse. Ralph Kimball famously stated:

"The grain is the binding contract on the fact table. The grain specifies exactly what an individual fact table record represents. Every candidate dimension and fact measure must be completely consistent with the declared grain." (Kimball & Ross, 2013)

Attempting to introduce dimensions or measures that do not conform to the declared grain results in catastrophic modeling failures, such as fan-out Cartesian joins, duplicate counting of physical quantities, or severe data corruption.

In the electric power generation and environmental monitoring domain, data naturally exists at multiple distinct grains that cannot be forced into a single monolithic table without destroying either temporal resolution or facility specificity:

  • Continuous smokestack emissions (EPA CAMPD) are captured at the unit-hour grain.
  • Fuel consumption and generation accounting (EIA-923) are captured at the facility-month grain.
  • Transmission grid demand and dispatch (EIA-930) are captured at the balancing-authority-hour grain.

To respect the natural grain of each source while enabling unified cross-domain analytics, our design defines three dedicated fact tables representing three distinct Kimball fact table archetypes.

2. Formal Fact Table Grain Declarations

1. FACT_HOURLY_EMISSIONS_WEATHER (Transactional Fact)
Formal Grain Declaration:
"One row represents continuous smokestack emissions, gross electricity output, thermal heat input, and nearest surface meteorological observations for one generating unit at a power plant during one clock hour."

Primary Key: Composite natural PK (date_key, hour_of_day, plant_key, unit_id)

Temporal Grain: Clock hour (0 to 23) within a calendar date (Smart integer YYYYMMDD).

Physical Entity Grain: Individual smokestack continuous emission monitoring system (CEMS) generating unit (unit_id) linked to parent plant (plant_key).

Spatial Meteorological Resolution: Spatially matched to the nearest NOAA synoptic surface station (station_key) within ≤ 50 km and ± 60 minutes.

Cardinality & Volume: Highest volume table in the warehouse. Exceeds 100,000+ fact rows across active thermal generating fleets.

2. FACT_MONTHLY_PLANT_OPERATIONS (Periodic Snapshot Fact)
Formal Grain Declaration:
"One row represents cumulative monthly net electricity generation, thermal fuel consumption, physical fuel quantity, operating heat rate efficiency, delivered fuel cost, and ending stockpiles for one power plant facility, prime mover technology, and energy source category during one calendar month."

Primary Key: Surrogate key snapshot_key (BIGINT IDENTITY). Logical uniqueness enforced on (month_start_date_key, plant_key, prime_mover_code, energy_source_code).

Temporal Grain: Calendar month (represented by month_start_date_key = first day of the month, e.g., 20230101).

Physical Entity Grain: Facility-level cross-tabulation of prime mover (e.g., ST, GT, CT, CA) and energy source fuel category (e.g., NG, SUB, BIT).

Legitimate Negative Measures: Net generation (net_generation_mwh) can legitimately be negative when an offline power plant consumes auxiliary station service power from the grid during maintenance. This is a valid physical event.

3. FACT_REGIONAL_GRID_DEMAND (Aggregate Grid Fact)
Formal Grain Declaration:
"One row represents macro real-time electric demand, day-ahead forecasted demand, net electrical generation categorized by six primary fuel technologies, and net interchange power flows for one regional balancing authority during one clock hour."

Primary Key: Composite natural PK (date_key, hour_of_day, ba_key).

Temporal Grain: Clock hour (0 to 23) within a calendar date (Smart integer YYYYMMDD).

Physical Entity Grain: Entire regional transmission balancing authority territory (e.g., PJM, CAISO, ERCOT, MISO).

Categorical Fuel Mix Granularity: Six mutually exclusive generation channels: Coal, Natural Gas, Nuclear, Hydro, Wind, Solar, plus Net Interchange.

3. Fact Measure Additivity Classification Matrix

Kimball dimensional modeling classifies numeric fact measures into three strict categories of additivity: Fully Additive (can be meaningfully summed across all dimensions), Semi-Additive (can be summed across some dimensions such as geography or units, but never across time), and Non-Additive (cannot be summed across any dimension; must be recomputed as ratios or averaged).

fact_hourly_emissions_weather gross_load_mw Fully Additive SUM() across units, plants, and hours. None.
fact_hourly_emissions_weather heat_input_mmbtu Fully Additive SUM() across units, plants, and hours. None.
fact_hourly_emissions_weather co2_mass_tons Fully Additive SUM() across units, plants, and hours. None.
fact_hourly_emissions_weather so2_mass_lbs, nox_mass_lbs Fully Additive SUM() across units, plants, and hours. None.

fact_monthly_plant_operations fuel_consumed_mmbtu Fully Additive SUM() across plants and months. None.

fact_regional_grid_demand net_gen_*_mw Semi-Additive SUM() across BAs for same hour; AVG() over time. SUM() across hours distorts dispatch power.
fact_regional_grid_demand net_interchange_mw Semi-Additive SUM() across BAs for same hour (net sum = 0). Summing across time obscures flow directionality.

5. Cross-Grain Reconciliation & Cartesian Trap Prevention

A common pitfall in multi-grain dimensional warehouses is the Cartesian Join Trap (or Chasm Trap), which occurs when an analyst attempts to join two fact tables at different grains directly in a single SQL query (e.g., joining fact_hourly_emissions_weather directly to fact_monthly_plant_operations on plant_key). Because one monthly plant row matches 744 hourly unit rows, measures from the monthly table will be multiplied by 744, producing massive overcounting.

Architectural Rule for Multi-Grain Reconciliation: To reconcile or compare data across different grains, queries must always pre-aggregate each fact table to the common grain within independent Common Table Expressions (CTEs) before joining: 

-- CORRECT: Pre-aggregating hourly fact to monthly grain before joining
WITH epa_monthly AS (
    SELECT plant_key,
           SUBSTRING(CAST(date_key AS VARCHAR), 1, 6) AS yyyymm,
           SUM(gross_load_mw) AS epa_gross_mwh
    FROM gold_warehouse.fact_hourly_emissions_weather
    GROUP BY plant_key, SUBSTRING(CAST(date_key AS VARCHAR), 1, 6)
)
SELECT p.plant_name, e.yyyymm, e.epa_gross_mwh, m.net_generation_mwh AS eia_net_mwh
FROM epa_monthly e
JOIN gold_warehouse.fact_monthly_plant_operations m 
  ON e.plant_key = m.plant_key
JOIN gold_warehouse.dim_plant p 
  ON e.plant_key = p.plant_key;
